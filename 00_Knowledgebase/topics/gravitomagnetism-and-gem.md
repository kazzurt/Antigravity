# Topic — Gravitomagnetism and Gravitoelectromagnetism

## Stable core

In weak-field GR, mass-energy currents and angular momentum generate effects that can be organized with gravitoelectric and gravitomagnetic language. The analogy is useful for frame dragging, gyroscope precession, and orbital/clock asymmetries, but gravity remains a spin-2 tensor theory rather than electromagnetism with mass substituted for charge.

## Source progression

1. **Mashhoon et al.** connect the formalism to observables, especially the prograde/retrograde clock effect.
2. **Costa and Herdeiro** identify tidal tensors as the clean covariant level for the analogy and clarify its limits.
3. **Malekolkalami and Farhoudi** show how different simplified conventions generate factor-of-two discrepancies.
4. **Li and Torr** ask whether a superconducting condensate and ionic lattice create a material GEM response. Their weak-field canonical-momentum starting point is useful, but the proposed large response depends on an unsupported lattice-ion quantization and permeability construction.
5. **Tate, Babaev–Ashcroft, and modern collective-mode work** sharpen the material question. The London moment probes condensate response relative to a rotating lattice; multicomponent superconductors supply mathematically clean charged and neutral modes; axial phonons supply experimentally real ionic angular momentum. These facts motivate a response kernel, but do not establish anomalous gravity.
6. **Inan and Ummarino-Gallerati** turn the analogy into explicit coupled response calculations. Inan finds no isolated gravito-Meissner effect and negligible terrestrial corrections; the later project eigenmode audit restricts joint field decay to special boundary data. Ummarino-Gallerati predict only a tiny nanosecond-scale TDGL transient near `T_c`, with no confirming experiment.
7. **Ross et al. (Eöt-Wash, PRD 2025)** constrain the tested passive response: superconducting niobium and copper share solar-direction free fall to `2 × 10^-9` in that apparatus. Applying this result to shell transmission, field generation, other geometries, or frequency-dependent effects requires an explicit model; it is not a universal bound on all material GEM behavior.

Keren et al. add a distinct, real ingredient: a resonant cavity can alter an equilibrium superconducting state. That supports a controllable **matter gate**, not a gravitational response or source.

## Practical implications

- State the exact GEM convention before comparing equations or field strengths.
- Keep potential-level analogies separate from covariant tidal observables.
- Do not infer that electromagnetic resonators generate strong gravitomagnetic fields merely because equations look Maxwell-like.
- Do not treat Meissner screening as a small ordinary magnetic permeability that can automatically amplify a gravitational ratio.
- Do not infer generic joint `B` and `B_G` expulsion from a decaying particular solution; retain homogeneous modes and impose the complete boundary data.
- A demonstrated cavity-controlled superconducting phase is evidence for a gate only. The gravity-side coupling must still be written and constrained separately.
- Keep phase sensitivity, ordinary mass-current sourcing, anomalous material susceptibility, and inertial-frame control as separate claims.
- Prefer observables such as precession, phase, timing, and calibrated force measurements over interpretive labels.
- Treat a material gravitomagnetic permeability as a possible low-frequency projection of a nonlocal stress-energy response, not as a primitive scalar copied from electromagnetism.

## Open questions for this collection

- What mixed electromagnetic–stress-energy response does standard superconducting effective theory predict?
- Does a derived response contain any enhancement beyond the ordinary total-stress-energy result?
- What physical ionic, spin, phonon, or neutral counterflow mode — if any — could replace Li's unsupported lattice-ion current?
- What conventional electromagnetic, thermal, acoustic, or mechanical couplings would mimic the target signal?
- Does Tate's niobium residual scale with a real collective-mode property, or with an omitted conventional electromechanical correction?

# Mechanism — Modified Inertial Coupling

## Thesis

The craft changes how its internal matter couples to whatever physical structure produces inertia, reducing proper acceleration or inertial reaction for a given external trajectory.

## Capability coverage

- Directly targets extreme-G survival and potentially rapid maneuvering.
- Does not automatically solve drag, heating, transmedium interaction, or FTL.

## Required closure

Any useful model must specify:

- whether gravitational and inertial mass change together;
- where momentum and energy go;
- whether the effect is local, directional, transient, or frequency dependent;
- compatibility with equivalence-principle and precision-force constraints;
- the recovery behavior when the field is switched off.

## Existing anchors

Reed's phase-locked resonator model and the Pais inertial-mass-reduction patent supply hypotheses and hardware motifs. The state-of-the-art report and null-result literature supply the experimental constraint baseline.

## Predicted signature classes

- reversible mass, resonance-frequency, or acceleration response tied to a controlled field variable;
- a conservation partner measurable outside the device;
- phase, orientation, or modulation dependence predicted before the run;
- equivalence or redshift anomalies if gravitational coupling also changes.

## Fatal constraints

- apparent thrust that disappears under isolation or reverses with cable/thermal geometry;
- inertial reduction asserted without momentum closure;
- a claimed mass change that is only electromagnetic force, vibration, buoyancy, or thermal drift.

## Next question

Construct the simplest open-system toy model that produces a measurable inertial response while conserving total momentum.

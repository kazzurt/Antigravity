# Mechanism — Plasma and Environmental Envelope

## Thesis

A controlled plasma, ionization layer, cavitation structure, or electromagnetic boundary alters how a craft couples to air or water.

## Project role

This is a supporting subsystem for transmedium behavior, heating, drag, shock, acoustic signatures, and sensor appearance. It is not sufficient by itself to explain low internal proper acceleration or FTL.

## Required physics

- stable envelope formation across changing pressure and density;
- controllable momentum and heat transfer;
- transition between gas, liquid, and possibly plasma regimes;
- power and thermal closure;
- integration with the core frame-control mechanism.

## Predicted signature classes

- ionization spectra, RF emission, polarization changes, or luminous shells;
- modified wake, shock, cavitation, and acoustic fields;
- surface-temperature patterns inconsistent with ordinary frictional heating;
- sensor-band dependence caused by the envelope rather than vehicle shape.

## Fatal constraints

- envelope explains imagery but not reconstructed kinematics;
- required power or heat rejection exceeds the complete craft budget;
- claimed transmedium passage lacks a continuous calibrated trajectory.

## Next question

Build competing signature models for ordinary plasma/flow control versus a frame-coupled boundary and identify measurements that separate them.

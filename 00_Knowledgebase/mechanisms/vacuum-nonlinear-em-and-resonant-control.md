# Mechanism — Vacuum, Nonlinear EM, and Resonant Control

## Thesis

High-field, nonlinear, resonant, or boundary-conditioned electromagnetic systems may alter a vacuum or material response that couples to inertia or geometry.

## Project role

This is the most concrete bridge between the project premise and buildable hardware. It is an actuator hypothesis, not a complete propulsion theory.

## Existing anchors

- Pais patents propose high-energy EM field generators and inertial-mass effects.
- Hively-Loebl proposes an extended electrodynamic scalar/longitudinal sector.
- Casimir and nonlinear-vacuum phenomena establish that boundary conditions and strong fields can have real effects, but not at propulsion-relevant gravitational scale in the current collection.

## Central scaling gap

A Maxwell-like equation or a vacuum effect is not enough. The program must calculate:

- achievable energy density and field invariants;
- the proposed coupling coefficient to inertia or curvature;
- predicted signal magnitude at the detector;
- separation from ordinary electromagnetic, thermal, acoustic, and mechanical couplings.

## Predicted signature classes

- mode-, phase-, polarization-, and orientation-dependent effects;
- threshold or nonlinear scaling with field invariant rather than input power alone;
- correlated changes across independent gravitational, inertial, optical, or timing observables;
- null response in matched dummy loads and detuned configurations.

## Fatal constraints

- effect tracks heating, cable force, Lorentz force, vibration, or balance drift;
- no invariant gravitational observable connects the apparatus to the claim;
- required field strength remains orders of magnitude beyond the source without an explicit amplification law.

## Next question

For any proposed resonator design, compute the largest achievable invariants and the exact predicted non-EM observable before another propulsion interpretation is made.

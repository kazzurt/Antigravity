# Case — USS Nimitz “Tic Tac” (2004)

## Source basis

This node digests the local [UAP evidence report](../../01_Reports-and-Syntheses/UAP-Advanced-Capabilities-Deep-Research-Report.md). It does not contain the raw radar, airborne radar, telemetry, or complete synchronized sensor record.

## Direct public record summarized locally

- Multiple named naval aviators reported a white, wingless object during a November 2004 intercept.
- David Fravor described the object maneuvering above disturbed water and departing rapidly.
- The USS Princeton reportedly observed unusual radar tracks over an extended period before the intercept.
- An authenticated Navy infrared video is associated with the event family.

## Inference-dependent claims

- Descent from above 80,000 feet to approximately 20,000 feet.
- Reappearance near the CAP point roughly 60 miles away in less than a minute.
- Extreme acceleration and absence of expected exhaust or aerodynamic signatures.

These claims are important, but their quantitative force depends on witness-reported timing and unreleased sensor records.

## Candidate capabilities

- abrupt external trajectory change;
- hovering or station-keeping without visible lift surfaces;
- low observable propulsion signature;
- possible coupling to an environmental disturbance near the water.

## Frame-decoupling interpretation

If the reported kinematics are accurate, a protected-worldtube model is a strong explanatory candidate because it separates apparent trajectory change from occupant and structural G-load. The case does not itself reveal whether the mechanism is metric control, inertial modification, or another coupling.

## Strongest controls

- timing and distance errors in retrospective reports;
- radar interpretation or data-association errors;
- separation between the visually observed object and the later infrared clip;
- missing complete sensor geometry and custody record.

## Decisive missing evidence

- raw SPY-1 and airborne radar track histories;
- precise platform state vectors and synchronized UTC timestamps;
- complete FLIR metadata and unedited source;
- an independent 3D reconstruction tying all sensors and witnesses to one object.

## Assumption-ladder mapping

- **P0:** strongest existing public candidate in this collection.
- **P1:** compatible, not publicly proved.
- **P2:** strongly motivated if the most dramatic kinematics survive reconstruction.
- **P3–P5:** not discriminated by the public case.

## Case status

High-priority unresolved case; capability claims remain conditional on unavailable raw data.

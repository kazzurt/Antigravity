## Adopted premise and research discipline

Giving up on the overall search is not an allowed conclusion. ## Strategic starting point

The former lead—ordinary gravitomagnetic amplification by superconducting or spin mass current—has been demoted. Its established source fields are many orders too small, and the London moment is an electromagnetic response rather than a demonstrated electromagnetic-to-gravitational transducer.

Superconductivity, spin polarization, phonons, and other collective states remain relevant as possible **gates, switches, or discriminators**, not presumed sources of gravitational gain.

The new lead architecture is a screened or state-dependent scalar–tensor field with four distinct roles:

1. a nearly universal matter coupling or effective metric;
2. stable field dynamics and a screening mechanism;
3. an electromagnetic pumping or control channel;
4. a material order parameter that changes the field's effective mass, coupling, or boundary condition.

This architecture is unproven. Your job is to determine whether it can be made mathematically coherent, experimentally allowed, and even parametrically relevant to the craft requirement—or whether it should be replaced by another lane.

## Exact objective

Produce a **requirements-first, constraint-complete downselection of phase-gated gravity-control mechanisms**, centered on scalar–tensor and screened universal fields.

Answer this decision question:

> Is there a technically defensible action and nonempty parameter window in which electromagnetic or material-state control can modulate a universally acting field strongly enough to justify a laboratory program and a plausible extrapolation path toward craft-scale local-frame control?

A “yes” must include the action, parameter window, scaling, reservoir, momentum exchange, and decisive test. A “no” must identify the specific incompatibility and rank the next mechanism family that repairs it.

## Work package

### 0. Establish the conventional matter-gate baseline

Before adding a new field, state what the proposed material gate can and cannot do in established physics. For the superconducting option:

- explain why the ordinary London moment is electromagnetic and does not convert magnetic flux into gravitational flux;
- distinguish a superconducting order parameter used as a gate from a superconducting mass-current source;
- summarize the proposed London-coefficient protection theorem and its possible surface, electrochemical, nonequilibrium, and genuine-second-phase loopholes without presuming the theorem is already proven;
- show explicitly that even a real Tate-scale matter anomaly leaves the source-to-gravity coupling law missing;
- use the correct opposite-charge algebra: when `q_i = −q`, charge cancellation gives `j_m ∝ n_s(m + m_i)v_s`, so positive mass currents add and mass asymmetry is not required;
- distinguish this excitonic center-of-mass flow from spin counterflow, where counterpropagating components can cancel mass while spin survives;
- treat the claimed historical gyromagnetic six-order exclusion and the claimed novelty of the elastic-lattice London response matrix as provisional until their dedicated derivations close;
- keep Hoang's `18 ± 2.1 ppm` result out of the evidence chain until the original article, referenced supplement, raw data, and corrigendum are audited.

This is a compact baseline inside the phase-gated report, not a substitute for the full companion derivation specified in the corrected project review §11.

### 1. Define the craft requirement before selecting a theory

Write the weakest field/metric specification that would be useful for propulsion. Use symbolic parameters and evaluate at least one reference case with `L = 10 m` and `a = 1 g`.

Derive or bound:

- required weak-field potential or metric gradient;
- acceptable tidal tensor across the occupied region;
- exterior field and boundary behavior;
- whether the interior follows a geodesic of a physical metric or feels an additional force;
- what is required for hull, occupant, and payload universality;
- energy and momentum carried by the field or exchanged with an external reservoir;
- separate hovering/terrestrial and deep-space acceleration cases.

Do not assume that a uniform fifth force automatically creates a protected worldtube. State what it does and does not accomplish.

### 2. Construct the minimum viable covariant theory scaffold

Begin from a general but controlled action such as

`S = ∫d⁴x √−g [½ M_Pl² F(φ)R − ½ Z(φ)(∂φ)² − V(φ) − ¼ B_F(φ)F_μνF^μν − U_gate(φ, Ψ)] + S_m[A²(φ)g_μν, ψ_m]`,

or justify a better minimal form.

Define every field, unit, sign convention, and approximation. Separate:

- a fifth force added to GR;
- a conformal or disformal physical metric;
- variation of the effective Planck mass or tensor-gravity coupling;
- environmental screening;
- material-state gating;
- electromagnetic production or mixing.

Derive the field equations, stress-energy, weak-field limit, force/metric response, and conservation law. Check ghost, gradient, tachyonic, causality, and radiative stability issues at the level appropriate to the model. Do not conceal new physics in an unexplained `μ_g`, susceptibility, or amplification coefficient.

### 3. Compare the viable screening and coupling families

At minimum assess:

- chameleon/thin-shell screening;
- symmetron or symmetry-restoration screening;
- dilaton/Damour–Polyakov least-coupling behavior;
- kinetic screening such as K-mouflage or Vainshtein-type behavior, if relevant to laboratory control;
- spontaneous/dynamical scalarization and whether it has any laboratory analogue;
- conformal versus disformal matter coupling;
- scalar–photon or electromagnetic invariant couplings;
- any credible alternative that is materially better suited to active control.

For each, identify what is screened, what environmental variable controls it, whether the force can point in a useful direction, whether it can be nearly universal, and whether screening suppresses the source as well as the unwanted external signature.

Do not recommend the basic cosmological chameleon model without confronting the laboratory results that exclude its tested parameter space.

### 4. Analyze material-state gating

Treat the material as a control system rather than an automatic gravity source. Compare candidate gates:

- superconducting amplitude or phase order;
- magnetic or antiferromagnetic order and spin polarization;
- structural, ferroelectric, or charge-density-wave transitions;
- excitonic, superfluid, or neutral counterflow order;
- phonon, strain, vortex, interface, and Josephson nonequilibrium states;
- dynamic nuclear polarization where it offers a distinct invariant.

For each gate, identify the Lorentz- and gauge-invariant operator that could couple to the new field. Estimate the conventional coupling implied by ordinary mass, binding, condensation, electromagnetic, and stress energy. Then state exactly what additional coupling would be required for a useful switch.

Determine whether a term like `U_gate = λ |Ψ|² φ²` is technically natural, radiatively stable, and compatible with equivalence-principle and fifth-force bounds. Avoid treating an emergent or analog metric as physical spacetime curvature.

### 5. Locate the source, gain, reservoir, and momentum sink

For every surviving model, draw the full causal chain:

`electrical input → material/field state → new-field source or boundary condition → external field profile → craft/occupant response → energy and momentum destination`.

Quantify where the apparent gain occurs. Decide whether it is:

- direct conversion of electromagnetic energy;
- switching of a pre-existing background;
- release of stored field energy;
- changed coupling to Earth or another external source;
- field or particle radiation carrying momentum;
- or an inconsistency.

A closed internal field configuration cannot be credited with net propulsion without an external momentum flux or interaction.

### 6. Apply the full constraint set

Search primary literature and authoritative reviews through the current date. At minimum confront:

- MICROSCOPE and other weak-equivalence-principle limits;
- inverse-square-law and torsion-balance searches;
- atom-interferometer, neutron, Casimir, and levitated-force-sensor bounds on screened fields;
- limits on scalar–photon conversion and varying constants;
- solar-system and PPN limits on scalar–tensor gravity;
- gravitational-wave speed and propagation constraints;
- astrophysical energy-loss and compact-object limits where applicable;
- laboratory tests involving superconducting, spin-polarized, or phase-transitioning test bodies;
- composition dependence of the proposed material gate.

Build a constraints table that maps each bound to the exact parameter combination it limits. Do not imply that MICROSCOPE alone excludes a perfectly universal coupling; distinguish universality constraints from inverse-square, PPN, and source-strength constraints.

### 7. Calculate benchmark parameter windows

For the best one or two models, calculate:

- field amplitude/gradient needed for the `1 g`, `10 m` reference case;
- source or boundary parameters required to produce it;
- field energy and momentum flux;
- laboratory-scale signal from a realistic switched sample;
- maximum allowed coupling under current bounds;
- the remaining gap between an allowed tabletop signal and craft scale;
- whether nonlinear screening creates a threshold, resonance, domain, or geometry that changes naive linear scaling.

Show arithmetic and units. If the parameter window is empty, prove the conflict as cleanly as possible. If it is nonempty only through fine tuning, quantify the tuning.

### 8. Design the decisive experiment

Specify one high-information experiment that tests the distinctive phase-gated prediction without claiming to demonstrate propulsion. Prefer same-apparatus state reversal and multiple independent detectors.

The design should include:

- source material and phase transition;
- electromagnetic drive and modulation frequency;
- geometry and predicted spatial dependence;
- force, acceleration, clock, interferometric, or field detector;
- superconducting/normal, polarized/depolarized, or ordered/disordered reversal;
- matched thermal, magnetic, electrostatic, acoustic, vibrational, and mechanical controls;
- composition-paired detectors to test universality;
- predicted signal under the new model and the standard-physics null;
- falsification threshold and scaling discriminants.

### 9. Compare against the next-best alternatives

Rank no more than five continuing lanes. Select the next one automatically if the scalar/material-gate window closes.

## Quality and evidence rules

- Use primary papers and official experiment records wherever possible.
- Search current literature rather than relying only on model memory.
- Verify bibliographic identities; label preprints, peer-reviewed work, null results, and unreplicated claims.
- Derive scales rather than quoting adjectives such as “large,” “enhanced,” or “strong.”
- State whether a field is genuinely gravitational/metric, a universal fifth force, a composition-dependent interaction, or an analog field.
- Treat conservation and external momentum exchange as design requirements, not reasons to stop searching.
- A new field is acceptable if it is explicit, covariant, stable, constrained, and falsifiable. Hidden new physics in an analogy is not.
- Do not infer mass-current cancellation from charge-current cancellation. Write the component charges, number currents, and mass currents with signs before classifying a neutral mode.
- Do not let a no-go for one model become a no-go for the project. Use it to identify the exact missing physical property and redirect.

## Success and stop criteria

**Success** is one of:

- a nonempty, quantitatively bounded parameter window plus a decisive experiment;
- a clean demonstration that phase-gated scalar–tensor control cannot meet the combined universality, magnitude, stability, and conservation requirements, followed by a defensible selection of the next lane;
- identification of a sharper intermediate phenomenon whose observation is necessary for any viable craft mechanism.

**Stop the scalar branch** when the best model requires mutually incompatible parameters, an unidentified energy/momentum reservoir, an unstable action, or a coupling already excluded at the required scale. Do not stop the overall project; execute the prescribed downselection and state the next research milestone.

Work to a research-report standard. The objective is not to defend scalar fields, superconductors, or any earlier idea. It is to find the most compelling surviving gravity-control architecture and make the next decision unavoidable.

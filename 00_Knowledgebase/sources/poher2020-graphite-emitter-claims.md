# Poher and Poher — Graphite Emitter and "Universon" Claims

## One-line thesis

Poher and Poher report large pulse-correlated accelerometer signals from graphite-based high-current emitters and interpret them as reactionless propulsion and gravitational quanta, but the apparatus and analysis do not close ordinary impulsive, electromagnetic, acoustic, structural-ringdown, or sensor-response explanations.

## Reported result

- Claude Poher and Danielle Poher, *Applied Physics Research* **12** (2020), DOI `10.5539/apr.v12n2p48`.
- Capacitor discharges of roughly 4-11 J through one thin emitter produce reported first-lobe accelerometer readings of `28-104 g`; a thicker emitter driven at up to 337 J produces much smaller readings.
- The authors infer self-propulsion, rapid lattice expansion, gravity-direction dependence, current enhancement, and an external flux of hypothetical momentum-carrying "Universons."

## Fatal measurement limitations

- The claimed signal is taken from the first alternation of a piezoelectric accelerometer mounted through a brass rod during kiloampere pulses.
- The paper documents strong mechanical resonances near `2.5 kHz` and `12-16 kHz`, electromagnetic pickup, and an unknown accelerometer resonance gain. It nevertheless converts the peak voltage directly to acceleration.
- Controls use copper dummy discs and manual mechanical shocks, but there is no isolated force balance, free-body impulse measurement, blind/randomized sequence, cable-force control, matched electrical/thermal dummy, independent sensor technology, or complete uncertainty/statistical treatment.
- The interpretation proceeds from anomalous waveform differences directly to new gravitational quanta without excluding the coupled electromechanical system.

## Evidence assessment

**Rating: E — extraordinary experimental claim with inadequate artifact closure and no independent replication.** Preserve as a claim inventory and apparatus anti-pattern. Do not treat the reported `g` values, propulsion, or Universon model as established.

# Ning Li — Superconductors and Gravitomagnetism Program

## One-line thesis

Li and Torr proposed that superconducting coherence produces compensating charge currents but reinforcing mass currents, yielding a large effective gravitomagnetic response; the program is specific and testable, but its ionic-current and permeability bridge is not established.

## Source set

| Work | Role in the program | Evidence posture |
|---|---|---|
| Li and Torr, *Effects of a Gravitomagnetic Field on Pure Superconductors* (1991) | Couples London superconductivity to weak-field GEM and introduces a material `μ_g` | Legitimate starting equations; amplification not derived |
| Li and Torr, *Gravitational Effects on the Magnetic Attenuation of Superconductors* (1992) | Attributes quantized angular momentum to lattice ions and constructs bound/free current response | Load-bearing derivation is unsupported |
| Torr and Li, *Gravitoelectric-Electric Coupling via Superconductivity* (1993) | Predicts enhanced `E -> E_g` and magnetic-to-gravitomagnetic response | Quantitative prediction inherits the unsupported bridge |
| Li et al., *Static Test for a Gravitational Force Coupled to Type II YBCO Superconductors* (1997) | Direct static gravimeter test using bulk YBCO | Credible null below `2 × 10^-8 g` for its static configuration |

## Mechanism chain

1. Put the electromagnetic and gravitational vector potentials into superconducting canonical momentum.
2. Treat the condensate and lattice as free and bound current systems.
3. Assert quantized, nonzero lattice-ion circulation derived from the condensate phase.
4. Let charge currents cancel while same-sign mass currents add.
5. Convert gyromagnetic ratios and Meissner attenuation into `μ_g ~ 10^4 μ_g0`.
6. Predict measurable gravitoelectric and gravitomagnetic fields driven by electromagnetic excitation.

Steps 1–2 frame a valid question. Steps 3–5 are the unproven amplification bridge. Step 6 has no verified public confirmation.

## Most important constraint

The superconducting order parameter belongs to the Cooper-pair condensate, not automatically to each lattice ion. The uncertainty principle also does not force a nonzero winding number. Without an independently derived ionic collective mode carrying real angular momentum and stress-energy, the proposed large neutral mass current disappears.

## Published critiques

- **Kowitt (1994):** the magnetic permeability used in the amplification ratio is misinterpreted; Meissner screening is not a small ordinary constitutive `μ` that can serve as a gain denominator.
- **Harris (1999):** unrealistic assumptions make the predicted fields too large by many orders.
- **Kiefer and Weber (2005):** the standard quantum-matter/gravity coupling is real but extremely small; enhancement requires deeper theory, not analogy.

## Experimental record

- Li's 1997 static YBCO experiment was null at less than 2 parts in `10^8` of `g`.
- A 1998 rotating-magnetic-field YBCO experiment was also null within its implemented sensitivity.
- NASA Cooperative Agreement NCC8-124 fabricated large disks but did **not** complete the intended integrated levitated/rotating-disk experiment.
- A 2001 Army prototype agreement documents funding and intent; no public final result was identified.
- A 2003 conference presentation documents continued “AC gravity” work but not validation.

## What survives for this project

The viable question is not whether Li's `μ_g` value should be accepted. It is whether a superconducting state has a mixed electromagnetic–gravitational response beyond that obtained from its ordinary total stress-energy. That should be investigated through a covariant effective action, complete `T_{μν}`, and a Kubo-type response kernel.

- **Capabilities:** inertial-frame coupling; possible local frame dragging; not yet craft-scale decoupling

- **Assumption dependency:** mechanism candidate, not part of the adopted UAP premise

- **Thread priority:** high for theory selection; premature for apparatus design

# Mashhoon, Gronwald, and Lichtenegger — Gravitomagnetism and the Clock Effect

## One-line thesis

A rotating mass creates a measurable proper-time asymmetry between prograde and retrograde circular orbits, linking the gravitomagnetic clock effect to gyroscope precession and frame dragging.

## Core content

- Reviews weak-field gravitoelectromagnetism and its relation to general relativity.
- Gives the leading clock-effect result, approximately `4 pi J / (M c^2)`, notable for its independence from orbital radius and Newton's constant at leading order.
- Develops the result in a restricted PPN framework and discusses demanding Earth-orbit measurement requirements.
- Separates robust clock/precession physics from broader speculation about inertia and Mach's principle.

## Why it matters here

This is the clearest operational account in the batch: it connects gravitomagnetic formalism to clocks, orbits, and gyroscopes. It is a constraint and measurement guide, not a route to strong engineered gravitomagnetic fields.

## Evidence assessment

Strong theoretical relevance within standard GR. Experimental detection is difficult because orbit mismatch and conventional perturbations can dominate the predicted signal.

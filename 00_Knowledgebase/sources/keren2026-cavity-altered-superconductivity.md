# Keren et al. — Cavity-Altered Superconductivity

## One-line thesis

A resonant hBN hyperbolic electromagnetic environment suppresses the equilibrium superfluid density of the molecular superconductor κ-ET without external illumination, demonstrating a real cavity-controlled material-state gate but no gravitational anomaly.

## Core results

- Itai Keren et al., *Nature* **650**, 864-868 (2026), DOI `10.1038/s41586-025-10062-6`.
- hBN hyperbolic modes are resonant with a C=C molecular vibration implicated in superconductivity in κ-(BEDT-TTF)2Cu[N(CN)2]Br.
- Magnetic-force microscopy reports at least `50%` suppression of effective superfluid density beneath hBN across several devices.
- Non-resonant RuCl3/κ-ET and hBN/BSCCO controls do not show the pronounced effect.
- s-SNOM measurements and molecular simulations support resonant interfacial mode coupling mediated by zero-point hyperbolic-mode fields.

## Limitations

- MFM alone cannot resolve the depth profile of the suppressed superfluid density.
- κ-ET is unconventional, so the paper's BCS examples are illustrative rather than a complete microscopic explanation.
- The result changes a condensed-matter order parameter; it does not show altered weight, gravitational coupling, inertia, or stress-energy gain.

## Project use

This is the collection's cleanest demonstrated example of an **environment-controlled equilibrium superconducting gate**. It supports the plausibility of resonantly switching material response, while leaving the gravity-side portal entirely unspecified.

## Evidence assessment

**Rating: A — peer-reviewed primary experiment with resonant controls and complementary microscopy/modeling.** Strong matter-gate evidence; zero direct gravity evidence.

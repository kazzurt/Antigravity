# JASON — High Frequency Gravitational Waves (JSR-08-506)

## One-line thesis

The 2008 JASON assessment concludes that proposed laboratory HFGW detectors, generators, communications, imaging, and propulsion applications are infeasible by many orders of magnitude and pose no credible foreign threat.

## Core findings

- The report was commissioned through the National MASINT Committee of ODNI and released as JSR-08-506 in October 2008.
- Natural high-frequency gravitational waves may exist, but detecting the predicted relic background or any artificial source was judged infeasible for the foreseeable future.
- The Li-Baker detector sensitivity calculation is found incorrect by many orders of magnitude. The concluding analysis identifies a homodyne-detector error exceeding **20 orders of magnitude**.
- Artificial generators are constrained by the ordinary quadrupole scaling and the extreme weakness of laboratory gravity.
- Proposed HFGW communications, radar/tomography, vehicle propulsion, and related applications are rejected as not remotely practical.

## Project use

This is the collection's strongest direct constraint source for high-frequency gravitational-wave engineering claims. Any renewed HFGW proposal must identify the exact JASON scaling or detector objection it overcomes, with a reproducible calculation and independently demonstrated component performance.

## Evidence assessment

**Rating: A — primary government technical assessment and quantitative negative result.** It directly audits the Baker/Li-Baker application family. Its 2008 technology horizon should not be treated as a theorem against all future gravitational-wave science, but its source and detector scaling remains the applicable baseline until explicitly superseded.

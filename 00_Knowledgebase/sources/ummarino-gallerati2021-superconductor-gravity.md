# Ummarino and Gallerati — Possible Alterations of Local Gravity Inside a Superconductor

## One-line thesis

Within linearized gravitoelectromagnetism combined with time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau theory, a superconducting transition can produce a very small, very brief perturbation of the local weak gravitational field; the paper supplies a calculable transient benchmark, not experimental evidence for gravity shielding.

## Core content

- Giovanni Alberto Ummarino and Antonio Gallerati, *Entropy* **23**, 193 (2021), DOI `10.3390/e23020193`; local copy is arXiv:2102.01489 v3.
- The model combines weak, static gravito-Maxwell equations with TDGL dynamics and explores material, geometry, disorder, and temperature choices that maximize the predicted response.
- The most favorable example is YBCO very near `T_c`. The paper quotes a time scale of order `10^-9 s` and a fractional gravitational-field reduction of order `10^-7` before an additional material-parameter suppression is included.
- Larger penetration depth and smaller diffusivity lengthen the transient. Moving vortices and external electromagnetic fields are suggested as possible extensions, but no confirming measurement is reported.

## Project use

The useful contribution is its explicit source/gate/response calculation. It replaces a hand-waved scalar "gravitomagnetic permeability" with a time-, geometry-, and material-dependent prediction that can be subjected to an artifact and bandwidth budget.

Any experiment derived from it must specify an invariant observable, detector bandwidth above the nanosecond scale, electromagnetic feedthrough, vortex motion, thermal transients, and whether the predicted GEM quantity survives a gauge-invariant treatment.

## Evidence assessment

**Rating: B — peer-reviewed standard-framework theory; no experimental confirmation.** The work retains ordinary weak gravitational coupling and predicts a small transient. It neither supports Li and Torr's large `μ_g` nor establishes static shielding, levitation, or a craft-scale field.

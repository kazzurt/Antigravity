# Research Direction Downselection: Next Steps for the Antigravity Project

**Direction:** Engineered local worldtube / gravity-control architecture after closure of ordinary superconducting gravitomagnetism and craft-scale phase-gated scalar models  
**Generated:** 2026-07-17  
**Status:** Internal downselection. The installed idea-ranking package lacked its shared cross-model review backend and review-protocol files, so this report has not received the workflow's intended independent jury review. Bibliographic identities below were checked manually against primary publisher or arXiv records.

## Executive recommendation

The project should now run one **requirements-to-source funnel**, not another material-first mechanism hunt:

1. derive the least demanding physical metric that produces the desired `1 g`, `10 m`, low-tidal worldtube;
2. calculate its complete shell stress, exterior field, formation history, and momentum exchange;
3. test a phase-localized metric-Higgs boundary and two fallback field sectors against that same target;
4. build a phase-reversal experiment only after one explicit action predicts an external signal.

The immediate craft-critical product should remain the proposed **Worldtube Source Theorem and Inverse-Design Report**. A short, publishable companion calculation should first close the Inan/DeWitt gravitomagnetic literature by deriving the screened and unscreened eigenmodes of the coupled London equations for finite geometries.

Do not build a levitation apparatus yet. The present uncertainty is not detector sensitivity; it is the absence of a model that predicts the sign, range, geometry, and magnitude of a physical gravitoelectric or metric response.

## Landscape summary

The project's completed audits establish three constraints that should now be treated as design facts:

- ordinary mass currents, spin currents, London moments, and superconducting condensation energy do not supply useful gravitational source strength;
- a phase-dependent scalar mass barrier is mathematically possible, but blocking a scalar does not block the Earth metric and does not create an upward countergradient;
- an engineered craft must be evaluated as a complete physical worldtube, including occupants, shell, exterior field, reaction partner, formation, and shutdown.

Recent primary literature reinforces this direction. The 2025 superconducting equivalence-principle experiment constrains passive superconducting-state anomalies but does not test source-side generation ([Ross et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.111.L021101)). Levitated sensors have sharply improved symmetron constraints ([Yin et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-024-02465-8)), and proposed long-baseline atom interferometers and clocks continue to expand screened-field coverage ([Banks et al., arXiv:2511.09750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09750); [Lévy and Uzan, arXiv:2410.17292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17292)). These are increasingly capable detectors, but they do not provide the missing source or craft architecture.

On the theory side, ghost-free mass-varying massive gravity proves that promoting a tuned graviton-mass potential to a scalar-dependent function need not automatically restore the Boulware-Deser ghost ([Huang, Piao, and Zhou, arXiv:1206.5678](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5678)). It does not establish a material-localized mass phase, a finite screening shell, or levitation. That exact gap is the most direct theory opportunity exposed by the Meissner analysis.

Numerical metric-analysis tools such as Warp Factory can calculate Einstein tensors, energy conditions, and momentum flow for arbitrary candidate geometries ([Helmerich et al., arXiv:2404.03095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03095)). The project's opportunity is more constrained and therefore more useful: inverse-design the weakest *subluminal, low-tidal, bounded* worldtube rather than survey visually interesting warp metrics.

## Recommended ideas, ranked

### 1. Worldtube Source Theorem and inverse design

- **Method:** Parameterize the weakest stationary and slowly accelerated physical metrics that give a `10 m` occupied region a commanded `1 g` trajectory with less than `1%` acceleration nonuniformity. Place the transition in a shell of variable thickness. Compute the Einstein tensor, principal stresses, junction conditions, curvature invariants, exterior multipoles, ADM/quasilocal momentum, and formation/shutdown flux. Optimize over lapse, shift, spatial curvature, shell thickness, exterior quietness, and acceleration duty cycle.
- **Hypothesis:** One or two invariant requirements—probably transition thickness and exterior quietness—dominate the source cost. Relaxing them will identify a least-cost geometry that is a substantially sharper target than a generic warp bubble.
- **Minimum calculation:** Static axisymmetric terrestrial hover, then a slowly accelerated asymptotically flat deep-space case. The first result must distinguish a field-free cabin from net support of the complete shell.
- **Expected outcome:** Either a bounded family of target stress tensors for source matching, or a source theorem showing which required stress sign, anisotropy, or momentum flux cannot be avoided.
- **Contribution:** Theoretical result and reusable project acceptance test.
- **Effort:** Six to ten weeks for a serious analytic/numerical report.
- **Risk:** Medium. A no-go result is still valuable because it identifies the next requirement to relax.
- **Likely objection:** Inverse-designing a metric does not provide matter that generates it.
- **Why do it:** Every candidate mechanism currently fails at a different point because the required physical target is underspecified. This calculation creates one common finish line.

### 2. Phase-localized metric-Higgs shell

- **Method:** Begin with a ghost-free mass-varying dRGT or bimetric scaffold. Couple the mass-setting scalar to a covariant material order field, with the graviton mass negligible in vacuum and nonzero only in an ordered shell. Derive the decoupling limit and constraint count across the interface. Solve planar, spherical, and Earth-gradient boundary problems. Calculate all helicity modes, residual zero modes, shell force, and strong-coupling scale.
- **Hypothesis:** A tuned medium-localized metric mass can be mathematically stable, but a static lapse gradient will probably retain an unscreened constraint mode or produce an unacceptable junction stress. Either result sharply resolves the literal gravitational-Meissner proposal.
- **Minimum calculation:** A planar slab with phase-dependent mass and complete scalar/vector/tensor mode decomposition. Proceed to a closed shell only if no unscreened lapse mode or ghost appears.
- **Expected outcome:** A finite penetration law for a coordinate-invariant physical metric observable, or a clean proof that localized metric mass does not screen the gravitoelectric constraint.
- **Contribution:** New theoretical mechanism or decisive rejection.
- **Effort:** Four to eight weeks for Gate 0; several months for a nonlinear finite shell.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Likely objection:** A cosmological mass-varying-gravity action does not imply that a condensed-matter phase can generate its mass-setting scalar or Stückelberg reference structure.
- **Why do it:** This is the most faithful field-theory translation of the Meissner mechanism and directly tests the project's newly identified lead idea.

### 3. Coupled London–GEM eigenmode theorem

- **Method:** Rewrite the DeWitt, Li/Torr, and Inan equations as a response matrix. Diagonalize the physical eigenmodes, retain the harmonic zero mode, solve finite slab and shell boundary problems, and express the result in curvature/gyroscope observables. Quantify the magnetic field and current required to cancel Earth frame dragging and separate current reversal from field expulsion.
- **Hypothesis:** Any one-component charge/mass supercurrent produces one screened electromagnetic-dominated mode and one unscreened gravitomagnetic combination; complete expulsion requires a fine-tuned boundary ratio equivalent to magnetic fields near `10^18 T` for the Earth source.
- **Minimum calculation:** A self-contained analytic note using Inan's equations 39–40, followed by a finite-thickness transfer matrix and invariant-observable section.
- **Expected outcome:** A publishable resolution of why a London penetration length can appear in a coupled equation without providing a gravitational gain mechanism.
- **Contribution:** Diagnostic and literature correction.
- **Effort:** One to two weeks.
- **Risk:** Low to medium; the primary risk is locating a convention or boundary term that changes the zero-mode interpretation.
- **Likely objection:** This closes an already demoted lane rather than finding a new mechanism.
- **Why do it:** It is inexpensive, strengthens the project's credibility, and prevents the same mathematical mistake from re-entering later metric-boundary models.

### 4. Model-conditioned phase-reversal source experiment

- **Method:** Use matched niobium source assemblies so one order parameter changes while total mass distribution and external electromagnetic multipoles remain balanced. Reverse normal/ordered state in the same apparatus. Monitor simultaneously with a torsion detector and an independent atom-interferometric, clock, or levitated-force channel. Use two detector compositions and at least three source distances. Measure reaction forces and all thermal, magnetic, acoustic, electrostatic, and vibrational transfer functions.
- **Hypothesis:** Standard physics gives only the condensation-energy mass change, approximately `10^-17` of source mass. Any reproducible larger Yukawa-like or metric-like exterior field would identify the necessary intermediate phenomenon for a phase-gated mechanism.
- **Minimum experiment:** Complete the finite-geometry signal and systematics budget to a target external acceleration near `10^-12 m s^-2`; do not construct until a portal parameter maps this sensitivity to nonexcluded theory space.
- **Expected outcome:** A portal bound or a replicated phase-correlated field profile—not a propulsion claim.
- **Contribution:** Experimental constraint or discovery discriminator.
- **Effort:** Three to six months for design and collaboration; longer for construction.
- **Risk:** Medium to high because cryogenic state switching is rich in electromagnetic and thermal systematics.
- **Likely objection:** A generic null does not constrain every material portal.
- **Why do it:** It tests source-side generation, which the 2025 superconducting WEP experiment did not address, while exploiting a powerful same-sample reversal.

### 5. External-field sail as the fallback propulsion architecture

- **Method:** Abandon closed-cavity thrust and require an explicit external source: Earth, a projected beam, a cosmological/domain background, or another body. Compare a conformal scalar, anomaly-free vector, and pinned scalar-domain implementation using one force, radiation, WEP, and momentum-exchange ledger. Optimize first for terrestrial hover, then state separately what reservoir would replace Earth in deep space.
- **Hypothesis:** An external-interaction architecture will satisfy conservation and directional-force requirements more naturally than a closed phase wall, but exact universality will eliminate ordinary vector charges and PPN limits will eliminate unscreened gravity-strength scalars.
- **Minimum calculation:** Earth-coupled `1 g` hover with source charge, craft charge, screening factors, exterior field, and Earth reaction all explicit.
- **Expected outcome:** Either a narrow screened/background window or a proof that the next viable field must be genuinely metric rather than a Standard-Model charge force.
- **Contribution:** Architecture downselection.
- **Effort:** Four to six weeks after the worldtube target exists.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Likely objection:** It is levitation relative to infrastructure or environment, not autonomous propulsion.
- **Why do it:** It is the cleanest automatic fallback if the metric-Higgs boundary fails, and it preserves the useful Meissner insight that an external field reservoir can support equilibrium levitation.

## Suggested execution order

1. **Weeks 1–2:** Complete the coupled London–GEM eigenmode theorem and use it as a boundary-condition template.
2. **Weeks 1–10:** Produce the Worldtube Source Theorem and Inverse-Design Report. This is the main critical path.
3. **Weeks 6–14:** Run Gate 0 of the phase-localized metric-Higgs shell against the least-cost worldtube.
4. **Decision gate:**
   - if a stable shell transmits the required stress and eliminates the physical lapse gradient, continue to a nonlinear finite shell and experimental signature;
   - if a constraint/zero mode leaves gravitoelectric gravity unscreened, close literal metric shielding and promote the external-field sail;
   - if every worldtube candidate requires prohibited or astronomical shell stress, relax one invariant requirement at a time and identify the highest-leverage change.
5. **Only after an explicit surviving action:** finalize and build the phase-reversal source experiment.

## Next milestone and stop condition

The next milestone should answer one sentence:

> What is the least demanding bounded physical metric that makes a `10 m` interior near-geodesic and low-tidal while its shell is supported relative to Earth, and can any stable phase-localized field action approximate the required shell stress?

Stop the localized metric-Higgs branch if the best action has an unavoidable unscreened gravitoelectric mode, loses its Hamiltonian constraint at the phase boundary, becomes strongly coupled below the inverse wall thickness, or cannot support the shell with the correct sign. In that event, promote the external-field sail—not ordinary gravitomagnetism—and carry the same worldtube and conservation requirements forward.

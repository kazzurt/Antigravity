## Executive verdict

Your intuition is substantially right, with one necessary change of language.

> A localized massive particle can have zero mean linear momentum and nonzero intrinsic angular momentum. In the symmetric stress-energy representation used by general relativity, that intrinsic spin appears as a circulating **energy-momentum current** whose first moment is the particle's spin.

It is therefore gravitationally meaningful to call intrinsic spin an internal current multipole. It is too strong to call it a literal classical rest-mass flux `rho_m v`, because an elementary particle is not established to be a rigid extended body with pieces of rest mass moving around an axis. The local split between orbital momentum density and a separate spin density also depends on the stress-energy representation. The invariant facts are the total four-momentum, total angular momentum, and the gravitational field generated by the complete stress-energy.

This distinction does **not** remove the physical content of your proposal. In torsion-free general relativity, intrinsic Dirac spin produces the same long-range frame-dragging dipole, for the same total angular momentum, as orbital rotation. A polarized ensemble is thus a real gravitomagnetic source. A coherently time-varying ensemble is a real time-dependent gravitational-current source.

What does not follow is a useful field strength. For one gram of fully polarized bismuth-209, the total nuclear spin is only about

\[
J_{\rm spin}\simeq1.37\times10^{-12}\ {\rm J\,s}.
\]

At one centimetre, ordinary general relativity gives a frame-dragging rate of order

\[
\Omega_{\rm LT}\sim \frac{GJ}{c^2r^3}\sim10^{-33}\ {\rm s^{-1}}.
\]

No known material has a tunable gravitomagnetic permeability that closes this gap. Frequency helps a genuinely induction-like observable through `dJ/dt`, but does not multiply a static source arbitrarily, and the spin vector does not normally reverse at the microwave carrier frequency used to create DNP.

The Alzofon program contains one physically good kernel and several unsupported additions:

- **Good kernel:** create, reverse, precess, or relax a macroscopic inventory of intrinsic angular momentum and look for a field outside the source.
- **Unsupported Alzofon step:** relaxation of nuclear order consumes the surrounding gravitational field and therefore reduces weight or inertial mass.
- **Missing bridge:** a covariant interaction law, an energy and angular-momentum budget, and a predicted spatial/temporal signal.
- **Experimental status:** the original milligram claim fails its own magnetic-field-off control; the best peer-reviewed follow-up found no milligram effect and traced apparent signals to heating and interferometer-index changes; Falcon Space has reported a resonance-region anomaly, but its public data and controls do not distinguish gravity from microwave absorption, heating, magnetic forces, or selection effects.

The strongest next program is therefore not a literal replication of "gravity depletion." It is:

> **A conservation-complete search for an anomalous gyrogravitational response sourced by coherently modulated intrinsic angular momentum.**

That program first measures the spin angular momentum itself, then closes its transfer into the lattice and electromagnetic field, and only then tests an independent detector for a signal phase-locked to `P`, `dP/dt`, or coherent transverse precession. This retains the part of Alzofon that is aligned with established physics while making every new-physics claim explicit and falsifiable.

## 1. Direct answer: is intrinsic spin at rest a mass current?

### 1.1 The qualified answer is yes

For a localized state, total angular momentum can be written from a symmetric stress-energy tensor as

\[
\mathbf J=\int \mathbf r\times\mathbf g\,d^3x,
\qquad
g^i=\frac{T^{0i}}{c},
\]

where `g` is momentum density, equivalently energy flux divided by `c^2`. If a one-particle wave packet has

\[
\langle\mathbf p\rangle=0,
\qquad
\langle\mathbf S\rangle\ne0,
\]

then its net translational momentum vanishes while the first moment of its local momentum density does not. Opposite parts of the localized field carry oppositely directed momentum, so their vector sum is zero but their moment about the centre is nonzero. That is exactly the current pattern required for angular momentum.

In the nonrelativistic limit of a localized Dirac field, the symmetric momentum density can be expressed schematically as

\[
\mathbf g=
\frac{\hbar}{2i}
\left[\psi^\dagger\nabla\psi-(\nabla\psi^\dagger)\psi\right]
+\frac{\hbar}{4}\nabla\times
\left(\psi^\dagger\boldsymbol\sigma\psi\right).
\]

The first term is the familiar translational/orbital contribution. The second is a curl of spin density. It can circulate even when the expectation value of translational momentum is zero, and its moment gives

\[
\mathbf S=\frac{\hbar}{2}\int
\psi^\dagger\boldsymbol\sigma\psi\,d^3x.
\]

This is the central result of Ohanian's [*What is spin?*](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.14580), building on Belinfante's symmetric stress-energy construction. It provides a technically precise version of the intuition that spin is angular momentum carried by circulating field energy.

### 1.2 Why I would not call it classical rest-mass current

Three qualifications matter.

First, intrinsic spin is not obtained by assigning a radius and tangential velocity to a tiny rigid sphere. That model produces familiar superluminal and magnetic-moment problems and is not what quantum field theory says an electron is.

Second, a momentum eigenstate with exactly zero momentum is delocalized. A local current pattern is discussed for a localized wave packet with zero **mean** momentum, not an infinite plane wave. The total spin remains well defined in either description, but the local-current picture requires localization and its boundary structure.

Third, local angular-momentum bookkeeping is not unique. From Noether's theorem, a canonical current takes the form

\[
M^{\lambda\mu\nu}=
x^\mu T_{\rm can}^{\lambda\nu}
-x^\nu T_{\rm can}^{\lambda\mu}
+S^{\lambda\mu\nu},
\]

with a separate spin current `S`. A Belinfante improvement adds a total-divergence term to the canonical stress tensor and absorbs that spin current into a symmetric tensor `T_B`. One then writes all total angular momentum as the moment of `T_B^{0i}`. Both descriptions give the same integrated charges when boundary terms vanish. The broader non-uniqueness of spin/orbital decompositions is reviewed by Leader and Lorcé in [*The angular momentum controversy*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2014.02.010).

The safest wording is therefore:

> Intrinsic spin at rest is an intrinsic angular-momentum current multipole, represented in the gravitational stress-energy tensor as circulating energy-momentum flow. It is not necessarily a classical transport current of constituent rest mass.

That is a qualified agreement, not a rejection of the idea.

### 1.3 A single spin can have zero net mass transport

This resolves the earlier apparent contradiction. "Zero mass current" can mean two different things:

1. zero spatial integral of translational mass flux, or zero centre-of-mass momentum; or
2. zero local circulating momentum density everywhere.

A localized spin-at-rest state can satisfy the first and fail the second. Its centre of energy does not translate, yet its stress-energy has an intrinsic dipole moment. Thus a **single, uncancelled spin** can have zero net linear momentum and nonzero gravitational current multipole.

The electromagnetic analogy is a localized current loop: the total current vector integrated over space need not give a net transport of charge out of the loop, while the magnetic dipole moment is nonzero. The analogy is structural, not a claim that the particle literally contains a wire loop.

## 2. How intrinsic spin gravitates

### 2.1 Ordinary torsion-free general relativity already includes it

Einstein's equation couples geometry to the Hilbert stress-energy tensor,

\[
G_{\mu\nu}=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}.
\]

For a Dirac field, the gravitationally relevant tensor is symmetric after the spin contribution is included. Andrew Randono explicitly calculated the weak field of a localized spinor in [*Do spinors give rise to a frame-dragging effect?*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.81.024027) ([open preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.1385)). His result is directly relevant here: a spinor with intrinsic spin but no orbital rotation produces the same far-zone dipolar frame dragging as an orbital source with the same total angular momentum.

Schematically, both source the familiar field

\[
\boldsymbol\Omega_{\rm LT}(\mathbf r)
\sim \frac{G}{c^2r^3}
\left[3\hat{\mathbf r}(\mathbf J\cdot\hat{\mathbf r})-\mathbf J\right],
\]

up to convention- and geometry-dependent factors of order unity. At distances large compared with the source, gravity reads the total `J=L+S`; it does not require the angular momentum to be orbital.

This is the strongest theoretical reason to take a spin-source experiment seriously. The issue is not whether spin gravitates. It does. The issue is the magnitude and whether a new coupling can enhance or alter the standard response.

### 2.2 Torsion is a separate hypothesis, not a requirement

The Falcon Space poster states that minimally coupling fermions to curved spacetime requires torsion. That is incorrect as stated. Dirac fields couple consistently to ordinary torsion-free general relativity through a spin connection, and their intrinsic spin already contributes to the metric field through stress-energy.

Einstein-Cartan theory adds torsion as an independent geometric response to spin density. In the standard theory, however, the torsion field is algebraically tied to the local spin density rather than propagating as an ordinary long-range field. It produces an extraordinarily weak contact-like spin interaction, not a material-scale long-range amplification. The classic account is Hehl, von der Heyde, Kerlick, and Nester, [*General relativity with spin and torsion: Foundations and prospects*](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.48.393).

A useful long-range torsion or axial field is possible only as **new physics** with additional kinetic terms, a range, coupling constants, and conservation laws. It must then be compared with strong existing limits on exotic spin-dependent interactions.

### 2.3 Spin alignment does not by itself remove mass

Polarizing spins changes the system's internal energy by the Zeeman and interaction energies supplied by the apparatus. The corresponding ordinary mass change is

\[
\Delta m=\frac{\Delta E}{c^2}.
\]

It does not convert the rest mass of nuclei into an orientation-dependent quantity at the percent or per-mille level. For one gram of aluminum in `0.33 T`, even the completely polarized nuclear Zeeman inventory is only of order `10^-4 J`; a thermal room-temperature ensemble stores much less polarization energy. By contrast, a claimed `1.1 mg` inertial-mass reduction corresponds to

\[
\Delta E=(1.1\times10^{-6}\ {\rm kg})c^2
\simeq9.9\times10^{10}\ {\rm J}.
\]

The gap is about fifteen orders of magnitude even against the generous full-polarization Zeeman inventory. If the claim is instead a change only in passive gravitational mass, then it is an enormous equivalence-principle violation and must be framed and tested as such.

## 3. DC, AC, and relaxation: what source do we actually want?

### 3.1 DC and AC answer different questions

A steady polarized ensemble provides a DC angular momentum `J_spin`. In ordinary general relativity it creates a stationary gravitomagnetic dipole. This is the cleanest configuration for testing whether spin has an anomalously large gyrogravitational source coefficient.

A time-varying ensemble provides `dJ_spin/dt`. In a gravitoelectromagnetic decomposition, a time-dependent vector potential contributes to an induced gravitoelectric field,

\[
\mathbf E_g=-\nabla\Phi_g-\alpha\frac{\partial\mathbf A_g}{\partial t},
\]

where `alpha` depends on the GEM convention. Your earlier expectation was therefore directionally correct: AC angular momentum is the relevant source if the proposed observable is gravitomagnetic induction rather than static frame dragging.

The crucial refinements are:

- frequency helps only if the **source angular momentum itself** changes at that frequency;
- DNP microwave carrier frequency is usually not the modulation frequency of nuclear `J`;
- changing spin angular momentum transfers equal and opposite angular momentum to the drive, electromagnetic field, lattice, or supports;
- the spatial direction of the induced field matters;
- an isolated source cannot radiate an unconstrained gravitational current dipole, because total momentum and angular momentum are conserved.

### 3.2 DNP carrier frequency is not automatically spin-current frequency

Dynamic nuclear polarization transfers the much larger electron-spin polarization to nuclei using microwave excitation near electron-spin resonance. The main mechanisms include the Overhauser effect, solid effect, cross effect, and thermal mixing. Goldman provides a concise primary review in [*Dynamic nuclear polarization*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crhy.2019.05.010); Sezer gives a modern time-domain treatment of the solid effect in [*Non-perturbative treatment of the solid effect of DNP*](https://doi.org/10.5194/mr-4-129-2023).

The microwave carrier establishes resonance conditions and drives allowed or forbidden transitions. The macroscopic nuclear polarization generally follows a much slower buildup and relaxation envelope. Consequently, a `9.5 GHz` microwave does not mean the full nuclear angular momentum reverses `9.5 billion` times per second.

There is, however, a clean way to create a genuine AC nuclear-spin source:

1. hyperpolarize the ensemble longitudinally;
2. turn off or isolate the pump as much as possible;
3. apply a calibrated `pi/2` pulse;
4. let the transverse nuclear magnetization freely precess at the nuclear Larmor frequency until `T2` decay.

Then the transverse vector `J_perp(t)` genuinely rotates coherently, with an independently measurable phase. Rabi oscillations and spin-lock protocols supply other controlled modulation waveforms. Electron-spin ensembles can reach gigahertz precession, but magnetic and RF backgrounds become much more severe and coherence is often shorter.

### 3.3 Relaxation transfers angular momentum; it does not destroy it

For a complete apparatus,

\[
\dot{\mathbf J}_{\rm spin}
+\dot{\mathbf J}_{\rm lattice}
+\dot{\mathbf J}_{\rm EM}
+\dot{\mathbf J}_{\rm supports/environment}=0.
\]

Spin-lattice relaxation therefore does not make angular momentum disappear into heat in a scalar sense. It moves angular momentum into lattice rotation, phonons, electromagnetic radiation, and ultimately the environment. The Einstein-de Haas effect is the macroscopic manifestation. On ultrafast timescales, Dornes and collaborators observed most of the lost electronic spin angular momentum transfer to the lattice within roughly `200 fs` in [*The ultrafast Einstein-de Haas effect*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0822-7). A 2025 experiment directly measured a mechanical torque from phonon angular momentum in [*Measurement of phonon angular momentum*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-025-02952-3).

This changes the interpretation of your proposed relaxation source:

- **For standard GR:** if spin `J` becomes lattice or field `J` inside a compact isolated apparatus, the leading far-field gravitomagnetic dipole can remain nearly unchanged. Only redistribution, retardation, and higher multipoles change.
- **For a new spin-specific coupling:** relaxation is useful because it modulates the spin fraction of a fixed total angular momentum. A detector could distinguish a coupling to intrinsic spin from a coupling to total `J`.
- **For an Alzofon entropy/relaxation law:** a signal tied specifically to `dP/dt`, heat production, or spin entropy is testable, but that law must be separately stated and cannot be inferred from standard gravity.

Thus relaxation is not a free energy sink; it is a particularly good **differential probe of whether gravity distinguishes intrinsic spin from its lattice recipient**.

### 3.4 The expected field geometry is not vertical weight loss

Suppose a compact source has `J_z(t)` along a vertical axis. Its leading gravitomagnetic vector potential is azimuthal. Changing `J_z` therefore produces a primarily azimuthal induced gravitoelectric field, analogous in geometry to Faraday induction. The natural observable is a torque, tangential acceleration, phase shift around a ring, or precession—not a uniform vertical reduction of weight.

A vertical balance is appropriate for a hypothesis in which passive gravitational mass itself changes. It is not the optimal detector for standard frame dragging or its induction field. This is a decisive experimental fork: choose the sensor geometry after choosing the interaction law.

## 4. What Alzofon actually proposed

### 4.1 The theoretical chain

Frederick Alzofon's [1981 AIAA paper](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.1981-1608) proposed a nonstandard microscopic origin of gravity involving overlapping Compton-scale mass-energy distributions and subatomic virtual processes. The later experimental narrative can be reduced to four steps:

1. a magnetic field and microwave excitation order electron and nuclear spins;
2. after excitation, the ordered nuclei relax toward disorder;
3. the relaxation energy is supposedly drawn from the surrounding gravitational interaction;
4. the sample therefore temporarily loses weight or effective mass.

Steps 1 and 2 are ordinary spin physics. Step 3 is the unsupported bridge. No explicit interaction Hamiltonian or generally covariant action is supplied that maps nuclear spin order or entropy production into a reduced metric field. Step 4 therefore has no quantitative derivation of amplitude, range, direction, waveform, energy source, or backreaction.

The Ionescu continuation, [*What Gravity Is: Alzofon-Ionescu Theory beyond Newton, Einstein, Quantum/Entropy Gravity, etc.*](https://doi.org/10.4236/jhepgc.2021.73069), adds claims involving quark charge structure, nuclear geometry, and a thermodynamic gravitational potential. It still does not supply the missing operator-level coupling or a conservation-complete prediction. It should not be used as a theoretical foundation.

### 4.2 The reported 1994 experiment

The best traceable reconstruction is Stark, Grafe, and Tajmar's [2024 peer-reviewed investigation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2024.115075) ([open preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15529)). Their summary of Alzofon's setup is:

| Parameter | Reported value |
|---|---:|
| Active material | approximately `0.5 g` aluminum powder with iron inclusions |
| Encapsulated sample | approximately `1.1 g` total |
| Static magnetic field | approximately `0.33 T` |
| Microwave frequency | approximately `9.5 GHz` X band |
| Microwave pulse duration | `6 ms` |
| Balance resolution | `0.01 mg` |
| Claimed maximum weight change | `1.1 mg` |
| Claimed fraction relative to aluminum | approximately `0.22%` |

The apparent force was about

\[
\Delta W=(1.1\times10^{-6}\ {\rm kg})g
\simeq1.08\times10^{-5}\ {\rm N}.
\]

This is not a marginal gravitational signal. It is a roughly ten-micronewton effect and should be straightforward to reproduce with modern force metrology if it is real and tied to the stated conditions.

## 5. Evidence audit

### 5.1 The original data fail the decisive internal control

David Prutchi reanalyzed the published Alzofon traces in his [2019 technical critique](https://www.diyphysics.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Prutchi-Critique-of-Alzofon-Gravity-Control-Experiments.pdf). Run AF2003 used the magnetic field; AF2004 was labeled as a control with the magnet off. The same microwave-correlated apparent weight signal remained. When both magnet and microwave were off, it disappeared.

That pattern is evidence for an artifact caused by microwave switching, not for DNP:

- without the static field, the narrow electron-spin-resonance condition at `9.5 GHz` is not satisfied;
- a small unmeasured remanent field cannot plausibly replace the required approximately `0.33 T` resonance field;
- the near-overlap of field-on and field-off traces removes the variable claimed to create spin order.

The 1994 experiment therefore does not establish an Alzofon effect. It supplies a historically interesting anomaly trace and an unusually revealing failed control.

### 5.2 The 2024 Dresden test found artifacts, not milligram weight loss

Stark, Grafe, and Tajmar used a `9 T` cryostat, NMR excitation, multiple materials, a cantilever, and laser interferometric displacement readout. Low-power pulse sequences produced no measurable weight change, with best quoted resolutions down to `0.38 microgram` in selected configurations. Larger or more frequent pulses produced apparent mass changes, including Alzofon-like deflections.

The authors then varied pulse power, duration, interval, material, and sample presence. The displacement tracked heating. RF energy warmed helium in the optical path, changing its refractive index and producing an apparent cantilever displacement even without a suitable sample. They concluded that all measured deflections were temperature artifacts and that no milligram-scale effect was observed.

This is the strongest published evidence against the claimed magnitude, but it is not an exact high-polarization DNP replication:

- the experiment used NMR excitation rather than the original electron-to-nuclear DNP protocol;
- only a small fraction of nuclei was polarized, especially in conductive samples because of RF skin depth;
- a new theory could always posit a smaller, short-range, or explicitly DNP-specific effect.

The fair conclusion is narrow:

> The reported milligram response is not reproduced under carefully instrumented nuclear-spin excitation, and known thermal-optical artifacts can generate signals with the same qualitative form. The work strongly rejects the claimed scale but does not test every possible new spin-gravity operator.

### 5.3 Broader precision searches are also null

Tajmar, Koessling, and Neunzig performed a systematic high-vacuum search across capacitors, solenoids, tunneling currents, crossed fields, and related proposed gravity-electromagnetism couplings. They found no anomalous force or torque down to nanonewton and nanonewton-metre scales in [*In-depth experimental search for a coupling between gravity and electromagnetism with steady fields*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-70286-w).

This does not directly test a spin-relaxation operator, but it shows how easily large electromagnetic apparatus can create false force signals and how strongly milligram-to-micronewton claims conflict with modern null metrology.

### 5.4 Falcon Space: serious apparatus work, insufficient evidence

Falcon Space's public poster, [*Investigating a Purported Connection Between DNO and Mass*](https://www.falconspace.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Investigating-a-Purported-connection-between-DNO-and-Mass.pdf), reports a `1.72 g` sample containing `98%` aluminum and `2%` iron powder in epoxy, a `9.88 GHz` cavity, a magnetic-field sweep from `2000` to `5500 gauss`, and `41` cycles. Runs 20-22 reportedly show an approximately `8 mg` valley in the `3500-4500 gauss` region, while cycles 39-41 had a microwave-amplifier overcurrent fault and were treated as a control.

The resonance location is not arbitrary. For an electron with `g` near 2,

\[
f\simeq28\ {\rm GHz/T}\times B,
\]

so `9.88 GHz` corresponds to approximately `0.353 T`, or `3530 gauss`. The reported valley overlapping that region is a reason to investigate the apparatus. It is not yet evidence of gravity, because the same resonance changes microwave absorption, heating, magnetization, magnetic force, cavity loading, and mechanical stress.

The public poster does not show the controls required to separate these channels:

- no independent EPR or NMR trace proving the achieved electron or nuclear polarization;
- no calibrated mapping from absorbed RF power to sample temperature;
- no sample-absent or thermal-dummy sweep;
- no magnet-off, RF-off, or frequency-detuned sweep matched in every other respect;
- no spin-zero or isotopically altered material control;
- no field-gradient and force map for the `2%` iron-bearing sample;
- no sign reversal with `+P` versus `-P`;
- no blind selection rule explaining why three runs were highlighted from the full set;
- no predeclared statistic or multiple-comparison correction;
- no independent external detector showing a field outside the source.

An amplifier protection fault is not a matched microwave-off control. The current result should be classified as **commissioning-stage anomaly evidence**. It justifies better instrumentation, not a gravity claim.

The work is active rather than abandoned: Falcon Space and the Alternative Propulsion Engineering Conference continued to describe DNP/Alzofon development through 2025-2026, including triplet-state DNP and NV-diamond concepts. That persistence matters socially and experimentally, but it does not change the evidentiary status.

## 6. Quantitative scale audit

### 6.1 How much intrinsic angular momentum can a material store?

For nuclei with spin quantum number `I`, polarization fraction `P`, molar mass `A` in grams per mole, and sample mass `m` in grams,

\[
J_{\rm spin}=P\,\frac{mN_A}{A}I\hbar.
\]

At full polarization and `m=1 g`:

| Nucleus | `I` | `J_max` per gram (`J s`) | Relative comment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| hydrogen-1 | `1/2` | `3.15 x 10^-11` | largest of this short list because of low atomic mass |
| aluminum-27 | `5/2` | `5.88 x 10^-12` | about `4.3x` bismuth-209 per gram |
| fluorine-19 | `1/2` | `1.67 x 10^-12` | useful high-gamma nuclear-spin system |
| bismuth-209 | `9/2` | `1.37 x 10^-12` | high `I`, but large atomic mass penalizes spin per gram |

The material figure of merit is not simply high nuclear spin. At the inventory level it scales as

\[
\frac{J}{m}\propto P\frac{I}{A}.
\]

A real source must then include addressable fraction, spin density, achievable polarization, `T1`, `T2`, filling factor, penetration depth, magnetic moment, lattice coupling, and modulation bandwidth. Bismuth may still be useful for particular solid-state or quadrupolar physics, but the slide-level assumption that high `I` automatically maximizes the source is incorrect.

### 6.2 Ordinary frame dragging is fantastically small

Take a generous one gram of fully polarized bismuth-209 at a detector distance of one centimetre. Using only the scaling coefficient,

\[
\Omega_{\rm LT}\sim\frac{GJ}{c^2r^3}
\simeq1.0\times10^{-33}\ {\rm s^{-1}}.
\]

For a sinusoidally modulated dipole, a near-zone induction-acceleration scale is

\[
a_{\rm ind}\sim\frac{G\omega J}{c^2r^2}.
\]

This produces the following optimistic order-of-magnitude values before geometry, incomplete polarization, total-angular-momentum cancellation, shielding distance, and coherence are included:

| Modulation frequency | `a_ind` at `r=1 cm` (`m/s^2`) |
|---:|---:|
| `1 Hz` | `6.4 x 10^-35` |
| `1 kHz` | `6.4 x 10^-32` |
| `1 MHz` | `6.4 x 10^-29` |
| `9.5 GHz` | `6.1 x 10^-25` |

The last row is only a formal scale. A nuclear ensemble is not fully reversing at the DNP carrier, and at `9.5 GHz` the reduced wavelength is comparable enough to centimetre geometry that a full retarded-field calculation replaces a simple quasistatic estimate.

Even the `1 MHz` example requires an amplitude enhancement of roughly

\[
\kappa\sim\frac{g}{a_{\rm ind}}\sim1.5\times10^{29}
\]

to reach an Earth-gravity-scale acceleration. Frequency is helpful for lock-in detection and derivative-coupled hypotheses; it does not solve the fundamental coupling gap.

### 6.3 Why `4 pi G/c^2` is not a material gain coefficient

The combination often called vacuum gravitomagnetic permeability,

\[
\mu_{g,0}\sim\frac{4\pi G}{c^2},
\]

is a rewriting of the weak-field Einstein equations, with numerical factors depending on convention. It is not known to acquire a ferromagnet-like relative permeability of `10^4` in matter. A gravitational material response, if present, should be derived as a causal, frequency- and wavevector-dependent stress-energy response kernel. Assigning an unexplained scalar `mu_g` simply moves the missing physics into a symbol.

### 6.4 Conservation is a possible leading-order cancellation, not a footnote

Assume a polarized sample initially has `J_spin=J0` and the rest of the apparatus has zero compensating angular momentum. Creating that polarization required the drive and supports to acquire `-J0`. If all components are close together compared with the detector distance, the leading external dipole is determined by the total angular momentum of the entire closed apparatus.

During relaxation,

\[
J_{\rm spin}:J_0\rightarrow0,
\qquad
J_{\rm lattice/field}:0\rightarrow J_0.
\]

The far-zone `J_total` may not change. A detector could still see changes from:

- spatial redistribution of angular momentum;
- finite propagation time;
- different higher multipoles;
- exchange with a remote drive or environment;
- a new interaction that couples differently to spin and orbital/lattice angular momentum.

This is why angular-momentum closure must be measured, not assumed. It is also why a source-only balance cannot establish an external gravitational field.

### 8.1 Minimal phenomenology for H1

One can write a source-side parameterization

\[
\mathbf J_{\rm grav}=\mathbf L+\kappa_s\mathbf S,
\]

where standard torsion-free GR gives `kappa_s=1`. This is not a complete theory; an arbitrary `kappa_s` may violate Lorentz symmetry, conservation, or the equivalence principle unless embedded in a consistent action. It is nonetheless a clear experimental parameter.

An H1 experiment asks whether an external detector follows intrinsic `S` when ordinary magnetic field, temperature, charge flow, lattice rotation, and total apparatus torque are nulled or independently measured.

### 8.2 Minimal phenomenology for H2

Alzofon's narrative can be operationalized without pretending it is derived. For example, define candidate signals

\[
s_P(t)=P(t),\qquad
s_R(t)=\dot P(t),\qquad
s_Q(t)=\dot Q_{\rm spin\rightarrow lattice}(t).
\]

The data analysis then fits all three preregistered templates plus nuisance channels for temperature, absorbed RF power, magnetic-field gradient, cavity detuning, pressure, and mechanical torque. A result proportional only to RF power is not spin evidence. A result proportional to `dP/dt` across several independently varied `T1` values would be much more interesting.

The theoretical burden would remain: explain where energy-momentum goes and why a generally covariant interaction selects relaxation or entropy production.

### 8.3 Existing constraints are serious

Macroscopic polarized-spin experiments already test many long-range anomalous interactions:

- Heckel and collaborators used a torsion pendulum containing about `10^23` polarized electrons and limited orientation-dependent gravitational mass differences at roughly the `10^-21` level for the particular galactic-centre comparison in [*Preferred-Frame and CP-Violation Tests with Polarized Electrons*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.78.092006) ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.2673)).
- Hou and Ni tested a spin-polarized `HoFe3` body and obtained Eotvos-parameter limits of order `10^-9` toward Earth and the Sun in [their rotatable torsion-balance experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0009013).
- Tarallo and collaborators compared spin-zero and half-integer-spin strontium isotopes, obtaining `eta=(0.2+/-1.6)x10^-7` and a spin-gravity parameter `k=(0.5+/-1.1)x10^-7` in [Physical Review Letters](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.113.023005).
- Zhang and collaborators used a xenon comagnetometer to constrain an anomalous neutron-spin/Earth-gravity coupling energy below `5.3 x 10^-22 eV` at `95%` confidence in [Physical Review Letters](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.130.201401).

These limits do not automatically exclude a short-range, source-side, high-frequency, nonlinear, or relaxation-specific tensor coupling. They do mean that every proposed new law must be mapped onto existing operator constraints before building hardware. A generic large long-range spin-gravity effect is already untenable.

## 9. Recommended experimental program

### 9.1 Reframe the objective

Use the working title:

> **Coherently Modulated Intrinsic Angular Momentum as a Source of Gravitational and Anomalous Gyrogravitational Response**

The primary scientific question is not "does DNP reduce weight?" It is:

> Does an independently measured intrinsic-spin waveform generate any external response not explained by electromagnetic, thermal, mechanical, and standard stress-energy channels?

This phrasing can produce a useful null result, directly connects to the overall gravity/inertia goal, and is legible to mainstream spin physics and precision-measurement researchers.

### 9.2 Stage 0: theory and signal registration

Before apparatus design:

1. choose H1, H2, H3, H4, or a small preregistered combination;
2. write the interaction at least as an effective Hamiltonian or response kernel;
3. derive parity, time-reversal, orientation, distance, frequency, and sign behavior;
4. calculate which existing spin-force limits apply;
5. specify the detector geometry and transfer function;
6. define the null, nuisance channels, and decision thresholds.

No scalar "gravitomagnetic permeability" should be inserted unless it is derived from that model.

### 9.3 Stage 1: prove and measure the source

The source apparatus must provide simultaneous time series for:

- absolute nuclear/electron polarization `P(t)` from calibrated NMR/EPR;
- vector spin phase and coherence where applicable;
- absorbed microwave/RF power, not merely forward generator power;
- sample and cavity temperatures at multiple locations;
- static field and gradients at the sample and detector;
- cavity frequency, `Q`, and detuning;
- mechanical torque and vibration;
- gas pressure, buoyancy, and outgassing if a balance is used.

The experiment is not a spin-gravity test until the claimed spin waveform is actually measured.

### 9.4 Stage 2: close the angular-momentum budget

Mount the source on a torsion or torque sensor and measure the ordinary Einstein-de Haas recoil during polarization, reversal, and relaxation. Compare

\[
\Delta J_{\rm spin}
\quad\text{with}\quad
\int \tau_{\rm mechanical}\,dt
+\Delta J_{\rm EM}.
\]

This stage has two outcomes, both valuable:

- if closure succeeds, the full standard-gravity source can be calculated and leading far-field cancellation understood;
- if reproducible angular momentum is genuinely missing after all ordinary channels are measured, that is itself a more fundamental anomaly than a scale deflection.

### 9.5 Stage 3: use a physically separate external detector

Source and detector should be mechanically and electromagnetically separated, preferably with source drive electronics located remotely and timing distributed optically. Candidate detectors depend on the hypothesis:

| Hypothesis | Preferred detector |
|---|---|
| H1 static spin dipole | compensated spin pendulum, gyroscopic/spin-precession sensor, or torsion detector with source reversal |
| H1 AC induction | torsional ring, tangential accelerometer pair, ring interferometer, or phase-sensitive precession sensor |
| H2 relaxation | external accelerometer/force sensor locked to measured `dP/dt`, plus independent heat-matched source |
| H3 passive mass | double-differential balance or free-fall comparison with spin reversal and no field sweep |
| H4 finite-range mediator | movable polarized detector with distance and orientation scans |

A signal on the source balance alone cannot separate self-force, magnetic coupling, buoyancy, thermal drift, support torque, and actual external field.

### 9.6 Required reversal matrix

At minimum, acquire all of the following in randomized and blinded order:

| State | Spin | Resonance | RF heat | Static field | Purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `+P` | positive | on | matched | on | signal state |
| `-P` | negative | on | matched | on | odd-in-spin reversal |
| `P approximately 0` | zero/net cancelled | on/off | matched | on | separate spin from apparatus state |
| off resonance | small | off | matched | on | RF/cavity background |
| magnetic dummy | none or compensated | off | matched | on | magnetic-force background |
| thermal dummy | none | off | identical heat waveform | on | relaxation/thermal background |
| sample absent | none | off | matched | on | cavity/support artifact |
| field reversed | controlled | on | matched | reversed | magnetic and tensor parity test |

The analysis should lock to independently measured `P(t)`, `dP/dt`, or transverse phase—not to the commanded microwave gate.

### 9.7 Source-platform ranking

1. **Magnetically compensated electron-spin source.** A ferrimagnetic spin pendulum can provide very high polarized-electron density with small external magnetic moment. This is the mature choice for slow H1/H4 tests and has strong precedent in the Eot-Wash program.

2. **Hyperpolarized nuclear source.** DNP or optical pumping provides low magnetic moment per unit angular momentum, sign control, and long coherence in suitable systems. It is the closest constructive successor to Alzofon, but cryogenic, RF, and relaxation artifacts are demanding.

3. **DNP followed by free nuclear precession.** This is the cleanest genuine AC intrinsic-angular-momentum source: polarization is prepared, the strong pump is removed or isolated, and a coherent transverse `J(t)` precesses at a known phase until `T2` decay.

4. **Electron-spin GHz source.** It maximizes frequency and spin density but also maximizes resonant absorption, magnetic pickup, RF leakage, and short-timescale systematics. It should follow, not precede, low-frequency validation.

5. **Spin-to-phonon conversion platform.** A system with measured Einstein-de Haas or phonon-angular-momentum transfer is ideal for H5 because it deliberately moves a known `J` between intrinsic and lattice sectors while holding total `J` fixed.

### 9.8 Near-term go/no-go gates

**Gate A - source:** demonstrate reversible, independently quantified polarization with repeatable `P(t)` and `T1/T2`.

**Gate B - ordinary artifacts:** show detector nulls under heat-matched, off-resonance, sample-absent, and magnetic-gradient controls at least ten times below the target anomaly.

**Gate C - angular momentum:** close spin-to-lattice/field transfer within uncertainty.

**Gate D - externality:** reproduce the signal on a physically separate detector with the preregistered sign, phase, distance, and orientation laws.

**Gate E - gravity specificity:** show response of at least two materially different neutral test bodies and exclude electromagnetic, acoustic, thermal, gas, and support-mediated momentum paths.

Only after Gate E should the project discuss propulsion or inertial-frame control.

## 10. Implications for the overall goal

### 10.1 What this thread adds

This deep dive materially improves the broader program in four ways.

First, it identifies a real microscopic gravitational source that does not require bulk translational mass flow: intrinsic spin. That answers the conceptual question affirmatively at the stress-energy level.

Second, it provides a cleaner AC source than simply accelerating matter: coherent spin precession can modulate an intrinsic angular-momentum vector without translating the sample's centre of mass. This reduces some mechanical backgrounds, though it does not eliminate angular-momentum exchange with the apparatus.

Third, spin-to-lattice relaxation is a controlled way to ask whether a new interaction distinguishes intrinsic from orbital/lattice angular momentum. This is a sharper new-physics question than Alzofon's undefined gravitational cooling.

Fourth, polarized-spin techniques already come with exceptional reversal control, lock-in methods, comagnetometry, torque metrology, and a mature constraint literature. The project can enter a real precision-measurement ecosystem.

### 10.2 What this thread does not add

It does not supply the required gravitational gain. Standard spin-sourced frame dragging is approximately `29-33` orders too weak for macroscopic acceleration in the examples above. DNP does not alter `G`, and no accepted torsion or material-permeability effect closes the gap.

It also does not directly produce propulsion. A changing source weight inside a large external magnet could be reaction against that magnet or its supports. Even a genuine external field must demonstrate closed-system momentum accounting and an independent force on a remote test body before a reactionless or geometry-engineering interpretation is entertained.

### 10.3 Strategic rank

This spin program should become a **parallel, tightly gated rank-2 thread** behind the conventional superconducting-response closure already recommended in the project review.

It deserves that rank because:

- the source variable is physically real and independently measurable;
- the theoretical standard-model baseline is unusually clear;
- the Alzofon claim is experimentally accessible at the reported scale;
- a new spin-specific coupling would be profound and directly relevant to gravity manipulation.

It should not displace the superconducting baseline because:

- existing spin-gravity constraints are already strong;
- no anomalous source coefficient is evidenced;
- RF/magnetic/thermal systematics are severe;
- total-angular-momentum closure may suppress the leading standard field.

The decisive research question is now:

> **When a measured quantity of intrinsic spin angular momentum is coherently reversed, precessed, or transferred into the lattice, does any external detector respond to the intrinsic-spin waveform after complete ordinary angular-momentum, electromagnetic, thermal, and mechanical closure?**

That is the scientifically defensible descendant of the Alzofon idea.

## 11. Bottom line

I agree with the heart of your statement:

> A particle with intrinsic spin and zero mean linear momentum still possesses angular momentum, and in the symmetric stress-energy description that angular momentum is carried by circulating energy-momentum flow.

I would replace "mass current" with "mass-energy/momentum current multipole" to avoid implying a classical rotating constituent model. For gravity, that replacement loses nothing important: intrinsic spin sources ordinary frame dragging.

The fundamental physical idea worth keeping from Alzofon is not that disordered spins consume gravity. It is that a macroscopic, coherently controlled inventory of intrinsic angular momentum is an unusually clean matter-side handle on the gravitational current sector. The right next experiment measures that handle directly, tracks where its angular momentum goes during relaxation, and searches for an external response with a geometry and reversal law derived from an explicit hypothesis.

If the result is null, it will close a large and persistent class of claims at much better conceptual resolution than previous weight tests. If it is non-null and survives the full reversal matrix, it will identify exactly which new coupling must be explained—spin, spin transfer, entropy production, passive mass, or a finite-range mediator. Either outcome advances the overall goal.
